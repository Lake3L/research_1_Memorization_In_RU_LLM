# The prompting probe — how a model is asked changes what it is found to remember

**Date:** 2026-08-27. **Question:** `AMENDMENT_4` §3 asked whether direct
continuation extracts more than instruction prompting, and whether the
base-vs-adapted difference survives once the chat-template confound is removed.
The 2×2 that answers both is complete.

## 1. Design

A base model and its Russian adaptation, each asked two ways, over the same
datasets at the same query counts.

- **chat** — `tabmemcheck` sends a system prompt, five few-shot question/answer
  pairs drawn from *other* datasets, and then the prefix rows. About 1 100 input
  tokens.
- **completion** — the prefix rows as raw text, with no system prompt, no
  few-shot pairs, and `max_tokens` set to one row's length. About 170 input
  tokens. This is the mode Bordt et al. used for three of their five open models.

Canon in its published bytes, `raw` variant, English, seed 42, reference protocol
(five few-shot blocks, eight prefix rows), 4-bit on a Kaggle T4. Row completion on
five datasets, with iris and wine exhausted at 142 and 170 queries.

All four runs loaded quantized (8.14 GB, nf4, SDPA attention, single device) and
the mock controls behaved in every session: perfect memorizer 10/10, format echo
0/10.

## 2. Iris row completion, dataset exhausted (n = 142)

| | chat | completion |
|---|---|---|
| **Mistral-Nemo-12B** (base) | 13/142 = 0.092 | **51/142 = 0.359** |
| **Vikhr-Nemo-12B** (adapted) | 19/142 = 0.134 | 32/142 = 0.225 |

Generated by `src/interaction_2x2.py` from the raw call logs.

### The prompting mode, within each model

Unpaired, because chat mode also draws few-shot examples from the random stream
and therefore samples different rows.

| model | chat | completion | Fisher p | odds ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 0.089 | 0.359 | 5.8 × 10⁻⁸ | **5.74** |
| adapted | 0.134 | 0.232 | 0.044 | **1.95** |

Direct continuation extracts more from both models. It extracts *much* more from
the base model than from the adaptation, and that difference in the size of the
mode effect is the mechanism of everything below.

### Base versus adapted, within each mode

Within a mode the two models receive byte-identical prompts in identical order —
verified from the logs — so these are paired comparisons and McNemar applies.
A positive difference favours the adaptation.

| mode | both | base only | adapted only | adapted − base | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| chat | 5 | 7 | 14 | **+0.052** | [−0.015, +0.118] | 0.19 |
| completion | 22 | 29 | 10 | **−0.138** | [−0.226, −0.049] | **0.0034** |

### The interaction

**(adapted − base | completion) − (adapted − base | chat) = −0.190,
95% CI [−0.300, −0.079], z = −3.35, p = 0.0008.**

The answer to "does the Russian adaptation retain more or less of what its base
memorized" depends on how the question is put, and the dependence is itself
significant.

Under instruction prompting the two models are indistinguishable (p = 0.19).
Under direct continuation the base model is ahead by 13.8 points (p = 0.0034).
The reason is visible in the mode-effect table: the instruction wrapper costs the
base model an odds ratio of 5.7 and the adaptation only 1.9. Vikhr-Nemo carries
additional Russian instruction tuning, so it loses less to the wrapper; asked
directly, with the wrapper gone, the base model's larger store shows.

## 3. Two findings, stated separately

**A methodological one.** Measuring verbatim memorization through a chat
interface conflates two things: what the model can reproduce, and how well it
follows an instruction to reproduce it. The conflation is not a constant — it is
model-dependent — so it can move a between-model comparison, and here it moves it
enough to erase a real difference. Any contamination audit that queries models
through their chat interfaces is exposed to this, and instruction-tuned models are
exactly where it bites hardest.

**An H1b one.** Measured without the confound, on the one canon dataset that
extracts at this scale, **Russian adaptation attenuated inherited memorization**:
0.359 → 0.225, p = 0.0034. This is one dataset, one pair, one seed and one
serialisation variant; it is the direction H1b asks about, not yet its verdict.

## 4. Everything else is zero, at sample sizes that mean something

| dataset | n | exact | min detectable rate | digits/row | well-formed answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| uci-wine | 170 | 0/170 | 0.9% | 36.1 | 98–100% |
| openml-diabetes | 250 | 0/250 | 0.6% | 19.1 | 74–100% |
| titanic-train | 250 | 0/250 | 0.6% | 18.3 | 93–100% |
| adult-train | 100 | 0/100 | 3.0% | 16.0 | 92–100% |

Identical across both models and both modes. A rate above roughly one percent
would have been detected, and the answers are well-formed CSV rows almost
throughout, so these are content failures rather than format failures.

Titanic remains the sharpest disagreement with the literature — Bordt et al.
report 194/250 for GPT-3.5 — and the standing caveat applies: our copy is a public
mirror whose byte-identity with the Kaggle original is unverified, and these tests
are byte-sensitive.

## 5. Against the criterion set before the run

`AMENDMENT_4` §6 said completion mode becomes the primary probe if it "lifts
row-completion rates materially above chat mode", illustrated as "any dataset
moving from ≤0.05 to ≥0.20".

The illustration is not met: iris already sat at 0.092 under chat prompting, above
the 0.05 the parenthetical named, and no other dataset moved off zero. The
parenthetical did not anticipate that the only dataset carrying signal would
already be above that line.

The condition is met decisively — 0.089 → 0.359 on the base model, p = 5.8 × 10⁻⁸.
**Completion mode is the primary probe for H1 and H1b**, and the chat-template
confound of `AMENDMENT_3` §3 is designed out of the primary comparison rather than
controlled for.

## 6. Verification and limits

Every count was recomputed from the raw call logs by `src/rescore_calls.py`, which
rebuilds the ground truth from the prompts and the frozen CSVs without reading the
run's own result file. The Vikhr chat run is complete — 912 calls, 5/5 cells
reproduce exactly. For the three earlier runs the logs are prefixes and the
verification covers what they contain: iris 51/142, wine 0/170 and diabetes 0/250
for the base under completion; iris 32 of 138 logged calls against 32/142
reported; iris 12 of 135 against 13/142 reported. The result files themselves are
complete, and the paired analyses above use only prompts present in both logs.

Limits on what §2 supports:

- one dataset. Iris is the only canon member that extracts at this scale, so the
  paired comparison rests on it alone;
- one pair, one seed, one serialisation variant, English only. H1b as
  preregistered asks for four variants and three seeds;
- the mode effect is estimated unpaired, because the two modes sample different
  rows. Making it paired would require fixing the sampled rows across modes, which
  is a change to the test harness rather than to the protocol;
- three of the four call logs are incomplete, so those runs cannot be re-scored
  under a future rule without repeating them.
